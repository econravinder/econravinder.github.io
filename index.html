About Me

Welcome to my webpage.

I am a doctoral candidate in the Economics and Planning Unit at the Indian Statistical Institute, Delhi Centre. My research interests lie at the intersection of Human Capital, Development Macroeconomics, the Economics of Education, Labour Economics, and the Rural Economy in India.

My doctoral thesis comprises three chapters on tertiary and foundational education in India.

  1. Transforming Rural Economies through Tertiary Education: Evidence from India. This chapter examines how college-educated individuals residing in rural India contribute to rural prosperity and, more broadly, to structural transformation.
  2. Foundational Skills and Labor Market Outcomes: Evidence from India. Here I ask whether a program that raised foundational learning in primary school also raised earnings in early adulthood, following the same birth cohorts from childhood test scores to young-adult labor market outcomes.
  3. Relaxing Entry Constraints: College Expansion and Tertiary Attainment in Rural India. The final chapter, my job market paper, asks whether the rapid expansion of colleges in Rajasthan after 2003 raised tertiary attainment among exposed cohorts, and what determined where the new colleges were established.

Methodologically, my work combines large-scale Indian administrative and survey data with applied econometric methods, including instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and staggered difference-in-differences and event-study designs, alongside machine-learning tools for prediction.
