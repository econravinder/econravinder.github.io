Relaxing Entry Constraints: College Expansion and Tertiary Attainment in Rural India

Download Paper (PDF) Slides (PDF)

Keywords: tertiary education institution expansion; educational attainment; land fractionalization
JEL Classification: I23, I25, I28, R52

Abstract

Tertiary education attainment among young adults in India has risen sharply over the past three decades, alongside a large expansion in the number of higher education institutions. This paper asks whether expanded access to colleges raised local rural tertiary education attainment. I study Rajasthan, which follows a similar trend to India in rural tertiary attainment but has experienced the highest rate of college expansion among Indian states. I combine geocoded college locations from the All India Survey on Higher Education with unique cohort-level records from the Socio-Economic and Caste Census 2011. Using individuals aged 22 to 31 in 2011, I construct a village-by-birth-cohort panel spanning 1998 to 2007, linking each cohort to the college supply prevailing when it reached college-entry age. The expansion began in 2003, and I exploit the staggered entry of colleges across villages. Exposure to a college within 15 km raised the tertiary attainment of exposed cohorts by about 1.4 percentage points two years after exposure and by 2.2-2.4 percentage points after four to five years, with no evidence of pre-trends. The effect is robust to several checks, including alternative comparison groups, distance thresholds, restricted cohort window, and adjustments for local economic activity, and holds for both men and women. Turning to college placement, district land fractionalization in 1999 is strongly associated with subsequent college growth. More- and less-fractionalized districts had similar college stocks before the expansion but diverged only after it began. This pattern is consistent with fragmented, lower-value agricultural land offering cheaper sites for a land-intensive, largely private expansion of higher education.

Key Findings

  • College access and attainment: Cohorts that reached college-entry age after a college opened within 15 km of their village were more likely to complete tertiary education than comparable cohorts in villages not yet reached, by about 1.4 percentage points two periods after access arrived and by 2.2-2.4 percentage points after four to five periods.
  • Women and men: The effect is significant for both women and men, and the average effect is almost identical across the two groups (1.25 and 1.24 percentage points), although their paths differ over time.
  • College placement: District land fractionalization in 1999, measured before the expansion began, is positively and significantly associated with subsequent growth in the number of colleges near a village at every bandwidth from 10 to 15 km. These estimates are interpreted as conditional correlations rather than causal effects.
